On a hill above Kurukshetra, a head keeps watch.
Not a ghost.
A promise.
His name was Barbarika.
Grandson of Bhima.
He carried three arrows—and a vow to side with the weaker army.
Krishna tested him with a smile.
"Can you pin every leaf of this peepal tree?"
One arrow rose.
Leaves fell like rain—except the ones Krishna had crushed under his foot.
Barbarika's second arrow hunted even those.
Krishna saw the truth.
This boy could empty both armies before dusk.
So Krishna asked for a gift.
Not his bow.
His head.
Barbarika knelt—and gave it—so he could see the war he was never allowed to fight.
From the hill, he watched brothers kill brothers.
And when the dust settled, he whispered what only the dead may know—
Victory has many hands.
But dharma has a cost.
